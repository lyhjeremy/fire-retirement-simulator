Would your plan have survived 1929, 1966 or 1973?

This simulator replays your plan through every stretch of US market history since 1871, or resamples that history into thousands of new paths, and finds the smallest change that gets the plan to 90%. Everything is in today's dollars: returns are after inflation, and spending, savings and pension hold their purchasing power.

Where the numbers come from

Returns are built from Robert Shiller's monthly data: S&P Composite prices and dividends, the CPI and the 10-year Treasury yield, January to January. His file currently runs to September 2023, so the last full year here is 2022 and the record covers 152 years from 1871. Bond returns are a standard approximation from the yield. The write-up has the method, a check against Damodaran's NYU Stern series and a check against the Trinity study.

What it leaves out

This is US history only, one of the best-performing markets of the period, so it is not a forecast. There are no taxes, fees, one-off costs or changes in spending with age, and the historical mode has only about 90 overlapping 63-year windows for the default plan. It is an educational tool and does not give financial advice.
