The chance your savings last, from 5,000 simulated markets

A rule like "save 25 times your spending" gives one answer for an average future. This plays your plan forward 5,000 times with a random return each year and counts how often the money lasts.

How a run works

Each simulation plays your life forward one year at a time. The portfolio grows by a random return drawn from a bell curve around the average and volatility you set; you add contributions until retirement, then withdraw your spending each year after. The success rate is the share of the 5,000 paths that never run out before your plan-until age.

Why not the 4% rule?

The 4% rule and "25 times your expenses" describe an average future, but you only retire once, and the order of good and bad years matters: a crash in the first years of retirement does far more damage than the same crash twenty years in. Replaying the plan across thousands of random futures turns a yes or no into a probability. This is a teaching tool, not financial advice.
